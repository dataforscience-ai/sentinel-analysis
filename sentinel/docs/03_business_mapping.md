## 3.2 Why a real organisation would collect this data, and what it reveals

- **Why collect it:** to measure fraud losses, to decide payments in real time, to explain declines to customers and analysts, to audit decisions for regulators, and to train and monitor models.

- **Insights it can give:** fraud rate by merchant category, channel, hour, region, device and amount band; how fraud relates to new devices, new cities, failed attempts and bursts; how long labels take to mature; and how reviewers' verdicts differ from the system's.

- **Patterns seen in the sample:** high-risk categories are gift cards (2.65% historical rate), gaming (2.13%) and electronics (1.48%), against about 0.02 to 0.03% for grocery, food, quick commerce, healthcare and travel. This matches the CRD's examples. Half of warm-up payments look like new devices and new cities, which is a start-up effect rather than risk.

- **Anomalies seen:** four failed attempts is the most common non-zero value, more than one, two or three; amounts and amount ratios have extreme tails; the validation sample has 42% of labels not eligible at the next split start.

- **Simulated system behaviour:** decision latency averages about 120 ms (maximum 220), inside the CRD's 250 ms; explanation latency averages about 0.9 s, so explanations must run off the critical path, as the CRD designs it. Train documents link to legacy cases, while validation and holdout documents link to transactions.

- **How it helps decisions:** it lets the organisation set thresholds from evidence, estimate review workload, and show why each payment was blocked or held.

- **Attributes expected to matter most (from the CRD; importance not yet tested):** fraud_label (target), amount_to_avg_ratio, is_new_device, customer_merchant_distance_km and is_new_city, hour_of_day and is_night, failed_transactions and has_recent_failures, account_age and is_new_account, txn_count_last_1h and txn_amount_last_24h_paise, merchant_category and its encoded rate, and distinct_customers_per_device_7d.
